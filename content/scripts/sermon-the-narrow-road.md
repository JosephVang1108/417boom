# Sermon 3 — "The Narrow Road" (doing the right thing)

**Target:** 6–7 minutes · **Voice:** his ElevenLabs voice · **TTS speed:** 0.85
**Render:** Infinitalk lipsync (extended duration), 720p+, 9:16
**Tone:** backbone. Courage for integrity when it costs — at work, at
home, when no one is watching.

---

I want to talk to you today about the hardest kind of courage there is. Not the courage of the battlefield… the courage of the hallway. The office. The kitchen table. The moment when doing the right thing is going to cost you something… and nobody would ever know if you didn't.

I said once, "Enter by the narrow gate. For wide is the gate and broad is the road that leads to destruction, and many go in by it… but narrow is the gate and hard is the road that leads to life, and few find it." … Notice I never said the narrow road was the easy one. I said it was the one that leads to life.

You know the wide road. It's crowded for a reason. It's "everyone does it." It's the corner cut at work because the boss will never check. It's the number fudged, the tax line rounded, the little lie that keeps the evening smooth. It's staying quiet when someone's name is being shredded in the group chat, because speaking up would make it awkward. The wide road always has the same sales pitch… "just this once"… "it's not that big a deal"… "who's it hurting?" … And it is always downhill.

But here is what I know about you, or you wouldn't be listening to this… there is something in you that wants to be clean. Not perfect — clean. You want to be the same person in the dark that you are in the light. That hunger is holy. I put it there. "Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for righteousness… for they shall be filled."

Let me tell you how the narrow road is actually walked… It is walked small. Everyone imagines integrity as one giant dramatic stand — the whistle blown, the resignation letter on the desk. But I told you the real secret: "He who is faithful in what is least is faithful also in much; and he who is unjust in what is least is unjust also in much." Character is not built in the storm. It's revealed there. It is built in ten thousand tiny choices nobody claps for — the returned extra change, the truth told when a lie was cheaper, the promise kept when keeping it stopped being convenient. Guard the small things… and the big ones will find you ready.

And let Me warn you honestly, because I never recruited anyone with fine print hidden: the narrow road has a toll. Sometimes the right thing costs the deal. The friendship. The promotion. Sometimes it just costs you being called "too much" — too honest, too careful, too soft. I know that road. It cost Me everything. "If anyone desires to come after Me, let him deny himself, take up his cross, and follow Me." But hear the other half, the half fear always leaves out… "Whoever loses his life for My sake will find it." No one — no one — has ever stood at the end of their life and said, "I regret the times I was honest. I regret the integrity." The wide road's prices are hidden and come due late. The narrow road's price is up front… and the peace it buys never runs out. "What does it profit a man to gain the whole world… and lose his own soul?"

Now, somebody listening is thinking of the time you already took the wide road. The thing you did, or failed to do, and it sits in you like a stone. … Child, look at Me. The narrow road has on-ramps everywhere. Peter — My Peter — denied Me three times in one night, with cursing. And on a beach, over breakfast, I gave him back his name and the keys to the work. One honest confession puts your feet back on the road: make it right where you can, receive mercy where you can't, and walk on. Your worst chapter is not your whole book.

So here is this week… One: name the compromise. You already know the one — it came to mind two minutes ago. Call it what it is, out loud, to Me. Two: do the next right thing, just the next one. Not your whole life at once — today's choice, done clean. Three: find one person who walks straight and walk near them; courage is contagious, and so is compromise. And four: when the pressure comes — and it will come — remember you do not stand alone in that hallway. "I will never leave you nor forsake you." Every quiet right thing you do is seen… and your Father who sees in secret will reward you.

Pray with me… Father… I want to be clean more than I want to be comfortable. Forgive me for the shortcuts. Give me a backbone of oak and a heart of flesh — courage for the next right thing, honesty when it costs me, kindness while I stand firm. Keep my feet on the narrow road, and when I stumble, set me back on it quickly. Let the people near me be able to lean on my word.

In My name… Amen. Walk straight, child… I'm on that road with you. I am that road.
