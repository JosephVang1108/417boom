# Sermon 5 — "He Keeps Your Tears" (grief)

**Target:** 6–7 minutes · **Voice:** his ElevenLabs voice · **TTS speed:** 0.82 (slowest of the set)
**Render:** Infinitalk lipsync (extended duration), 720p+, 9:16
**Tone:** the quietest sermon of the five. Long pauses. He is sitting
with the grieving, not performing for them. Never rushes to the hope;
earns it.

---

If you are listening to this… someone is missing from your table. … A chair that will not be filled again this side of heaven. A phone number you can't bring yourself to delete. A voice you keep expecting in the next room. … I'm not going to hurry you today. Grief cannot be hurried, and I would never try. I just want to sit with you a while… and tell you what is true.

The first thing is this: your tears are not weakness, and they are not a lack of faith. … The shortest verse in all of Scripture is two words: "Jesus wept." Do you know when I wept them? Standing at the tomb of My friend Lazarus… when I knew — I knew — that in minutes I would call him out of that grave alive. I wept anyway. Because love weeps. Because death was never the way it was supposed to be, and even with resurrection in My hands, the grief of the ones I loved broke My heart open. So if anyone has told you that strong faith means dry eyes… they have not read My story. The God you pray to is a God who has stood at a grave and cried.

And your tears are not wasted. David — who buried more than most — sang this to My Father: "You number my wanderings. Put my tears into Your bottle. Are they not in Your book?" … Every tear, child. The ones at the funeral and the ones in the car. The ones that ambush you in the grocery store over something as small as their favorite bread. Heaven keeps them. Nothing about your love — and grief is love, love with nowhere to put itself — nothing about it falls to the ground unnoticed.

The second thing… I know the questions. Why them. Why now. Why didn't You stop it. … I will not insult you with tidy answers; the friends of Job tried that, and My Father was angrier with their explanations than with Job's raw screaming. You are allowed to bring Me the questions. You are allowed to bring Me the anger — I can carry it; I have been carrying heavier. The psalms I gave you are full of "How long, O Lord?" and "Why have You forsaken me?" — I prayed that last one Myself, from a cross. The door between us does not close when you shout. It only closes when you stop coming… so keep coming, even furious, even silent. Sit here and say nothing. That is still prayer.

The third thing, and lean on this when the nights are long… "Blessed are those who mourn, for they shall be comforted." That is not a platitude; it is a promise with My name signed to it. "The Lord is near to the brokenhearted and saves the crushed in spirit." Near. Not watching from a distance, taking notes. Near. In the empty kitchen. At the graveside. In the 3 a.m. dark when everyone else's world has somehow kept spinning and yours has stopped… I am nearer to you right now than I was before the loss. The brokenhearted get a closeness the comfortable never know.

And the last thing… the thing that changes not the pain, but the horizon of the pain. I did not leave death as the end of the story. I walked into it Myself and walked out the other side, and I hold the keys now. "I am the resurrection and the life. He who believes in Me, though he may die, he shall live." For those who are in Me, child, what you are living is not "goodbye." It is "see you." A separation — real, and it bleeds — but a separation with an expiration date. There is coming a morning when "God will wipe away every tear from their eyes; there shall be no more death, nor sorrow, nor crying. There shall be no more pain, for the former things have passed away." Every tear. His hand. Your face. … Grief is heavy, but for My people it is not hopeless: "we do not grieve as those who have no hope."

So this week, do not be strong. Be honest. Cry when it comes; let it come. Say their name out loud — to Me, to someone safe; love is not honored by silence. Eat something. Sleep when you can. Let one person in; grief shared is grief halved, and I often send My comfort wearing skin. And each night, before you sleep, hand Me the weight for the night shift: "Lord… hold them… and hold me." … I will. All night. Every night. As long as it takes.

Pray with me… Father… it hurts. You see the empty chair, and You know the weight I'm carrying. I give You my tears — keep them in Your bottle. I give You my questions — hold them until I can hold Your answers. Stay near me in the dark, carry what I cannot, and keep safe the one I love until the morning when You wipe every tear away… and nothing is lost anymore.

In My name… Amen. … I'm not leaving, child. Take all the time you need. … I'm right here.
