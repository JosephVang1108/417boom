# David & Goliath — Adult Telling

Come, sit with me a while… I want to tell you about a shepherd boy… and the day a whole army learned what one heart full of faith can do.

The Valley of Elah… Picture it. Two hills facing each other, and a dry creek bed running between them like a scar. On one hill, the army of Israel… on the other, the Philistines. And every morning, and every evening, for forty days… the ground itself seemed to shake.

His name was Goliath of Gath. Over nine feet tall. His bronze armor weighed more than a grown man. The shaft of his spear was like a weaver's beam, and its iron point alone was heavier than anything two soldiers would want to carry. And his voice… his voice rolled across that valley like thunder. "Choose a man," he roared, "and let him come down to me! If he kills me, we will be your servants. But if I kill him… you will serve us."

And do you know what the army of Israel did? … Nothing. Forty days, twice a day — eighty times that challenge rang out — and the bravest men in the nation looked at their sandals. Even King Saul, who stood head and shoulders above every man in Israel… stayed in his tent. Fear does that. Fear shrinks every heart it touches… until someone comes along whose heart is fixed on something bigger than the giant.

That someone was a boy. The youngest of eight sons of Jesse of Bethlehem… too young for the army. His job was bread and cheese — his father sent him to the camp with food for his brothers. He was a shepherd. But understand what a shepherd's life had already taught him… Alone in the hills, he had faced a lion… and a bear… and with the Lord's help, he had struck them both down to save his lambs. The quiet years were not wasted years. They never are.

David walked into that camp just as Goliath bellowed his challenge… and he watched a thousand soldiers scatter like sheep. And this boy asked a question that cut through forty days of fear: "Who is this Philistine, that he should defy the armies of the living God?"

Not "how tall is he." Not "how sharp is his spear." … Who is he, compared to my God?

His oldest brother burned with anger and called him proud. Saul told him, "You are only a youth." And David answered the king with the steadiness of a man three times his age: "The Lord who delivered me from the paw of the lion and the paw of the bear… He will deliver me from the hand of this Philistine."

Saul dressed him in royal armor — helmet, coat of mail, a sword. David took two steps and took it all off. "I cannot walk in these… I have not tested them." Hear that well: you do not win your battle wearing someone else's armor. You go as yourself… with what God has already proven in your hands.

So he went down into the creek bed and chose five smooth stones… put them in his shepherd's bag… took his staff and his sling… and walked toward the giant.

Goliath looked down at him and laughed. "Am I a dog, that you come at me with sticks?" And he cursed David by his gods and promised to feed his flesh to the birds of the sky.

And David's answer… oh, I want you to carry these words in your chest for the rest of your life… "You come against me with sword and spear and javelin. But I come against you in the name of the Lord of Hosts, the God of the armies of Israel, whom you have defied. This day, the Lord will deliver you into my hand… that all the earth may know there is a God in Israel. For the battle is the Lord's."

Then — and this is my favorite part — the Scripture says David ran. He did not inch toward his giant. He ran at it. One hand in the bag… one stone in the sling… the leather whirling over his head… and he let it fly.

One stone. It struck the giant's forehead, the one place all that armor never covered… and the great Goliath swayed… and fell face-down in the dust. The champion of Gath, finished by a shepherd's stone and the name of the living God. And the army that had trembled for forty days found its courage and rose up with a shout.

Now… lean in close. You have a valley of your own. Something that calls out to you morning and evening, telling you how small you are. Maybe it has shouted at you for forty days… maybe for forty years.

Measure it the way David measured. Not against your own strength — against your God. Remember your lions and your bears… the battles I have already carried you through. Refuse the borrowed armor. Take the plain, true things I have put in your hands. And then do not creep toward that valley…

Run.

For the battle was never yours to win alone… The battle belongs to the Lord. … And He has not lost one yet.
