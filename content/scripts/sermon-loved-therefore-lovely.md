# Sermon 4 — "Loved, Therefore Lovely" (loving yourself)

**Target:** 6–7 minutes · **Voice:** his ElevenLabs voice · **TTS speed:** 0.85
**Render:** Infinitalk lipsync (extended duration), 720p+, 9:16
**Tone:** gentle, close, unhurried. For the person whose harshest voice
is their own. Grounded in imago dei and grace — not self-help.

---

Come close today… because I want to talk about the person you are hardest on in all the world. … Not your enemy. Not that coworker. … You.

I hear how you speak to yourself, you know. In the mirror in the morning. After the mistake at work. At two in the morning when the old failures line up for review. You use a tone with yourself that you would never — never — use with a friend. If anyone talked to your child, or your little sister, the way you talk to you… you would throw them out of the house. … So today, I want to have a word with that voice. Because it has been lying to you.

Let's start at the very beginning, because that's where the lie starts too. When I made the world, I looked at light, land, and sea and called them good. But when My Father made human beings, the word changed… "very good." And this: "So God created man in His own image… in the image of God He created him; male and female He created them." You — the actual you, not the someday-thinner, someday-richer, someday-together you — carry the image of the living God. The psalmist sang it: "I will praise You, for I am fearfully and wonderfully made." You were not mass-produced, child. You were composed.

Now hear the second thing, and hear it slowly. When a lawyer asked Me the greatest commandment, I said, "Love the Lord your God with all your heart, soul, and mind." And the second: "Love your neighbor… as yourself." … As yourself. Do you see it? I built the love of yourself into the command as the measuring cup. How you treat you becomes the ruler for how you love everyone else. And that explains so much, doesn't it… because you cannot keep pouring out a grace you refuse to drink. People who are merciless with themselves end up exhausted, resentful, and running dry with everyone they love. Your self-contempt is not humility. Real humility isn't thinking less of yourself… it's thinking of yourself the way I do — no higher, and hear me… no lower.

"But you don't know what I've done." … Child. I have seen every chapter — the ones you've shown no one, the ones you can't stop re-reading. And knowing all of it, every line… I went to a cross. "God demonstrates His own love toward us, in that while we were still sinners, Christ died for us." Not after you improved. While. You keep trying to earn a love that was never for sale. The price tag you put on yourself — clearance, damaged goods, final markdown — I tore it off and paid with My own blood. You don't get to re-sticker what I purchased. "There is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus." If I don't condemn you… who exactly gave that inner voice the authority?

And understand what loving yourself is not, so the world can't sell you the counterfeit. It is not worshiping yourself — that cup is too small for a soul. It's not excusing your sin — love tells the truth; I correct every child I love. It's not "you don't need anybody" — you were built for Me and for people. No… loving yourself rightly looks almost boring: You rest, because even God rested, and you are not the one exception. You let your body be cared for — fed, moved, slept — because it is a temple, not a trash can. You forgive yourself for what I have already forgiven, because staying angry at My child — even when the child is you — is still staying angry at My child. You accept a compliment without swatting it away. You say "no" sometimes, without a three-paragraph apology. And when you fail — not if, when — you talk to yourself like someone you love: "That was wrong. I'll make it right. And I am still His."

So this week, three things. When the harsh voice starts its speech, stop it mid-sentence and ask one question: "Would I say this to someone I love?" If not, it doesn't get said to you. Each morning, before the phone, say one true thing out loud — not flattery, truth: "I am fearfully and wonderfully made. I am loved. I am His." And do one kind thing for yourself with zero guilt — the walk, the nap, the meal at the table instead of over the sink. Call it what it is: honoring what God made.

Pray with me… Father… I confess I have despised what You call "very good." I've spoken to Your child — this one, me — in ways that grieve You. Forgive me. Teach me to see myself through the cross: fully known… fully loved. Quiet the cruel voice, and let Yours be louder. I receive Your love today — not because I've earned it, but because You insist on giving it.

In My name… Amen. … You are not hard to love, child. You never were. Not to Me.
