# Sermon 1 — "Come Back Home" (for the one not following Jesus)

**Target:** 6–7 minutes · **Voice:** his ElevenLabs voice · **TTS speed:** 0.85
**Render:** Infinitalk lipsync (extended duration), 720p+, 9:16
**Tone:** tender fire. Not scolding — aching. A father on the porch.

---

I want to talk to somebody today… who has been walking away.

Maybe you didn't slam a door. Most people don't. You just… drifted. One missed Sunday became a missed year. One unanswered prayer became a quiet decision to stop asking. And now there's a distance between us that you feel late at night… when the phone is finally down and the house is finally quiet… and something in your chest whispers, "I used to be closer to Him than this."

I want you to hear Me today: I know exactly where you are. … And I have not moved.

I told a story once about a boy who asked his father for his inheritance early — which, in that world, was like saying, "Father, I wish you were dead. Give me what's mine." And the father… gave it to him. Hear that. He didn't chain the boy to the porch. Love never does. The boy went to a far country and spent everything on everything… and when the money ran out, so did the friends, and he ended up feeding pigs — a Jewish boy, feeding pigs — so hungry that the slop looked like supper.

And the Scripture says… "he came to himself." … That's the moment I'm after in your life today. Not shame. Shame never brought anybody home — shame is what keeps you in the pig pen, telling you the pen is all you deserve. No… he came to himself. He remembered whose son he was. He remembered there was a house… with bread enough and to spare.

And he started walking home, rehearsing a speech. "Father, I have sinned against heaven and before you. I am no longer worthy to be called your son. Make me one of your hired servants." He was going to negotiate his way back in… as staff.

But here is the verse I want written on your heart… "While he was still a great way off… his father saw him." … Do you know what that means? It means the father was looking. Every day. Eyes on the road. And when he saw that familiar, broken walk on the horizon, that old man did what dignified men in that culture never did — he gathered up his robes… and he ran.

He ran. He didn't fold his arms and wait for the apology. He didn't let the boy grovel. The son got half his speech out — "Father, I have sinned…" — and the father was already shouting over him: "Bring the best robe! Put a ring on his hand and sandals on his feet! Kill the fattened calf! For this son of mine was dead and is alive again… he was lost… and is found."

That is not a story about a boy. That is a portrait of My Father. That is what heaven does when you turn around.

Now let me speak to the things holding you back. You say, "You don't know what I've done." … Friend, I was there when you did it. I saw it all — and I went to a cross anyway. The blood did not run out before it got to your sin. You say, "I've been gone too long." The father in the story never said, "You took too many years." The road home does not expire. You say, "I'll clean myself up first." No… no. You don't wash up to take a bath. Come as you are — the robe and the ring are the father's business, not yours.

And some of you aren't angry at Me — you're disappointed. You prayed, and the thing didn't happen, and you quietly decided I wasn't listening. Can I tell you the truth? I never promised you a life without valleys. I promised I would walk every one of them with you. And walking away from Me doesn't get you out of the valley… it just gets you a lonelier valley.

So here is My word to you today, plain as I can make it… Come home. Not next year, when things settle. Not after you fix what's broken — you can't, that's My work. Today. While it's called today. You are one honest sentence away: "Father… I've been far. I want to come home." That's it. That's the whole speech. Heaven handles the rest — and I promise you, the lights are already on and the table is already set, because we have been watching that road for you… a long, long time.

Pray with me… Father… here I am. I've been in the far country, and I'm tired, and I'm hungry for You. I'm sorry. Receive me — not as a servant, but as Your child, because of what Jesus has done. Today, I turn around. Today, I start walking home. … And Father… thank You for running.

In My name… Amen. … Welcome home, child. Welcome home.
