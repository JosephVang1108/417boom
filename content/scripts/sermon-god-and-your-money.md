# Sermon 2 — "God and Your Money" (finances)

**Target:** 6–7 minutes · **Voice:** his ElevenLabs voice · **TTS speed:** 0.85
**Render:** Infinitalk lipsync (extended duration), 720p+, 9:16
**Tone:** practical, dignified, hopeful. No prosperity-gospel promises;
no shame for the broke. Provision, stewardship, generosity, peace.

---

Let's talk about something that keeps more people awake at night than almost anything else… money.

The bills on the counter. The number in the account that never seems to stretch to the end of the month. The debt that follows you around like a shadow. The quiet fear of "what if" — what if the car breaks, what if the job goes, what if I never get ahead. … If that's you, breathe. You are not in trouble with Me for struggling. And you're not alone — I talked about money and possessions more than almost any other everyday thing, not because I wanted something from people… but because I knew what money tries to do to a heart.

Here is the first thing I ever want you to know: your Father sees your needs. On a hillside in Galilee I said it this way… "Look at the birds of the air. They don't sow or reap or gather into barns — and yet your heavenly Father feeds them. Are you not worth much more than they? … Consider the lilies of the field, how they grow. They don't toil or spin, yet Solomon in all his glory was not clothed like one of these." … So do not be anxious, saying, "What shall we eat? What shall we wear?" Your Father knows you need all these things. … Seek first His kingdom and His righteousness… and all these things will be added to you.

That is not a promise of a mansion. It is something better — it is a promise of a Father. The birds still go out and find the food; the promise is not laziness rewarded. The promise is that you are not out here alone, hustling against an indifferent universe. You are a child in a Father's economy… and He does not forget His children.

Second… let Me be honest with you, because I love you: money makes a wonderful servant and a terrible god. "No one can serve two masters… You cannot serve God and money." Notice I didn't say you can't have money. Abraham had wealth. Lydia had a business. The question is never what's in your account — it's what's on the throne. Here's how you know money has climbed up there: when it has your first thought in the morning and your last fear at night… when enough never arrives no matter what the number is… when you treat people as expenses or opportunities. I said it plainly: "One's life does not consist in the abundance of his possessions." You are not your net worth. You never were.

Third — be faithful with what is actually in your hand. Not the money you wish you had… the money you have. I told of a master who gave three servants different amounts — five talents, two, one — and the words he spoke to the faithful ones were identical: "Well done, good and faithful servant. You have been faithful over a little; I will set you over much." Faithful over a little. That's the whole exam. So count what you have — that's not unspiritual, I commended counting the cost before building a tower. Tell your money where to go instead of wondering where it went. Owe no one, as far as it lies with you — debt makes you a servant of a second master. Start small; heaven applauds small and faithful long before it notices big and careless.

And fourth… the strangest key in My kingdom: the way out of money's grip is open hands. "Give, and it will be given to you — good measure, pressed down, shaken together, running over." I watched a widow at the temple treasury drop in two small copper coins — everything she had — and I told My disciples she gave more than all the rich men that day. Why do I care so much about giving? Not because heaven has bills. Because every act of generosity breaks the lie that money is your security… and plants the truth that your Father is. Generous people are free people. I have never once met a giver with a hoarder's fear in their eyes.

So here is your walk this week. Thank your Father for what you have — gratitude starves anxiety. Look honestly at what's in your hand and make a plan for it, even a small one. Take one step off the throne-of-money — one debt attacked, one impulse refused. And give something — money, a meal, your time — to someone who cannot pay you back… and watch what it does inside your chest.

Pray with me… Father… You know my numbers, and You know my fears. I bring both to You. Teach me to seek Your kingdom first and to trust You for the rest. Make me faithful with the little, free from the love of money, and generous like You. Provide for my house, Lord… daily bread, open doors, wisdom in every dollar. I put You — not money — on the throne of my heart.

In My name… Amen. Your Father knows what you need, child. … Walk in peace, not in fear.
